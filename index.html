Why SpendGuard

Agents need to pay. Giving one your wallet key is how a small mistake becomes a total loss.

SpendGuard turns spending authority into something you can grant. The owner keeps the only full key. Every agent, bot, or cashier gets a session key that pays only approved recipients, only up to a cap, only until an expiry. The contract enforces every bound. A compromised key can lose at most its remaining cap, and the owner takes it back in one click.

Core capabilities

Bounded delegation, enforced by the contract

The interface shows state and submits transactions. It never authorizes anything. Every check lives in the vault itself.

Scoped session keys

Each key carries its own allowlist, per-payment limit, total cap, and expiry. A key is good for exactly one job. Nothing more.

Caps that cannot be bypassed

Every check runs on-chain before the transfer. No frontend, script, or prompt can talk the contract out of it.

Instant revocation

One owner transaction and the key is dead forever. A fresh grant starts at zero spent. A revoked key never comes back.

Full audit trail

Every deposit, grant, payment, revoke, and sweep emits an event. The full history of the vault is readable on-chain. No logins. No private database.

Guarantees

What the contract promises

Bounded loss, always

The worst case for one compromised key is its remaining cap. A stolen key, a malicious recipient, reentrancy, a frontrun: none of them turns a bounded loss into a vault drain.

Proven by INV-1, INV-9, INV-10 in the threat model.

Pause never blocks recovery

Pausing freezes every payment instantly. The owner sweep works in every state: paused, keys revoked, keys expired. Recovery is never locked behind the circuit breaker.

Proven by INV-4, INV-6 and adversarial test T-7.

No silent state changes

Every state-changing operation emits an event with full parameters. If it happened, it is on the chain, readable by anyone, forever.

Proven by INV-15; verified in the unit suite with event assertions.

How it works

Three steps to bounded spending

Aevum has no session-key tooling yet. The official SDK ships in Q1 2027, and no published bundler, paymaster, or factory is usable by builders today. SpendGuard is that missing piece, built in plain Solidity. It works on the testnet now, with a plain wallet.

1

Deposit once

Approve exactly the amount you want to deposit, then deposit it. The vault pulls exactly that amount. No unlimited approvals anywhere. It credits what actually arrived.

2

Grant a session key

Name the key, its recipient allowlist, its per-payment limit, its total cap, and its expiry. Review the summary, sign once. The key is live the moment the grant is mined.

3

The key pays, within bounds

The session key pays merchants itself. Every payment is checked on-chain: caller, active, unexpired, within caps, recipient allowlisted, vault funded, vault unpaused. Anything else reverts with a plain-language reason.

Revoke in one click

A revoked key can never spend again. Re-granting starts fresh at zero spent.

Expiry is a hard cutoff

A payment in the exact expiry second reverts. There is no grace period to exploit.

Testnet only, test tokens only

SpendGuard runs on the Aevum testnet with tUSD8 test tokens. No tokens are issued or promised.
